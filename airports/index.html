Part 139: class and index, explained

14 CFR Part 139 certificates an airport in two separate, independent ways: a Class (I–IV), for the kind of scheduled/unscheduled air-carrier service it is certificated to handle, and an ARFF Index (A–E), for the size of aircraft rescue and firefighting equipment it must keep on hand, sized to the longest aircraft that actually uses the airport. An airport has one of each -- they answer different questions and neither implies the other.

Class I–IV -- what service the airport is certificated for
ClassCertificated for
IScheduled large air carrier aircraft (31+ seats), plus any other air carrier operations (unscheduled large, or scheduled small)
IIScheduled small air carrier aircraft (10–30 seats), plus unscheduled large -- not permitted scheduled large
IIIScheduled small air carrier aircraft only
IVUnscheduled large air carrier aircraft only -- no scheduled service of any kind

Source: FAA, Classes of Airports faa.gov/airports/airport_safety/part139_cert/classes-of-airports, accessed 2026-09-27.

Index A–E -- what firefighting equipment the airport must keep on hand
IndexSized for aircraft length
Aunder 90 ft
B90 ft to under 126 ft
C126 ft to under 159 ft
D159 ft to under 200 ft
E200 ft and over

An airport is indexed to the longest air carrier aircraft type that regularly serves it -- except 14 CFR §139.315(c): once that longest aircraft type has fewer than five average daily departures, the airport may instead operate at the next lower index. Source: 14 CFR §139.315, law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/139.315 (Cornell LII mirror; ecfr.gov itself redirected to a bot-verification page on direct fetch), accessed 2026-09-27.

Does ICE Air concentrate at Class IV airports?

Class IV airports handle no scheduled service at all -- if ICE Air charter flights, which are themselves unscheduled, cluster there more than air traffic in general does, that would be a real finding about this network's own infrastructure footprint. Airports with no Part 139 certification at all

A different, more basic case than the flag above: an airport with no certification has no certificated index at all for anything to be measured against. 14 CFR §139.1(c)(2) categorically excludes airports operated by the United States (military airfields) from Part 139 regardless of what they serve -- expected, not a finding.
