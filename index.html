How to read this map ×

●Each dot is an ICE detention facility, colored by its role in the transfer network (see the legend, left) and sized by throughput -- total stints recorded there since 2025-01-20.
↻Each arc is a facility-to-facility transfer route, drawn thicker the more transfers ran along it. Every mapped route is drawn, down to a single recorded transfer.
▣Flight arcs are recorded ICE Air legs — brighter, thinner and higher than the transfer arcs, because a leg is an observed event where a transfer's mode is inferred. They depict aircraft, not people.
↗Arrest → first detention (off by default) shows where people enter the system: both ends recorded, the straight line between them not. Green, low, beneath everything else. Only an arrest with BOTH a recorded city and a linked first-detention facility can be drawn here at all -- 86% of all recorded arrests in the study window, but just 56% of arrests of people under 18 (the three filter buttons above the timeline all draw from this same restricted population). The Arrests stat box reports the true total regardless of whether a specific arrest can be drawn; its second line shows how many of those are actually shown as arcs.
✕Arrest only (no facility recorded) (off by default) shows the rest of those arrests -- a recorded city, but no linked first-detention facility, so no second point exists to draw a line to. A small X mark, not a filled dot, so it's never mistaken for a facility or for an arc's own endpoint. 73.5% of all arrests missing one piece of location data are missing the facility specifically, not the city -- 17x more lopsided for arrests of people under 18. Clickable for a per-case trace, the same as the arrow layer -- the trace is simpler (no stints, since none exist) but still shows the arrest, case status, and any recorded case-level departure.
↗Removal flights are the warmer arcs, drawn higher. Many leave the frame entirely — the map does not stop at the border, because the operation does not.
△Airports are drawn as small hollow triangles, not circles -- a different kind of node from a facility. Off by default, like the federal circuit boundaries; switch both on in the panel at left. Click an airport for its recorded flight legs, carriers, and aircraft types.
□Interstate highways can be switched on as quiet ground context for the routes.
▣Hotspot (weekly change) (off by default) opens its own popup with every one of this layer's controls: the color legend, three selectable baseline methods (flat average, flat average with a 1-week guard gap, and an exponentially weighted average -- hover any button for exactly how that one works), a week scrubber, and Play. Each county is shaded not by raw volume but by how its current week compares to the selected baseline — blue below it, red above, off-white near it. Counties with too little volume under the selected method show gray, "not enough data for a trend," rather than a wild percentage computed on almost nothing. Press Play to step through the whole study window automatically; the currently-shown week is also marked on the main timeline below (a small white triangle, distinct from that timeline's own window handles). Read this carefully regardless: a direct check found that every one of the 25 highest raw-count counties has 66–100% of its arrests recorded in a single city that hosts an ICE hold room, processing center, or detention facility — a week's change here can be a change in processing/hold activity, not necessarily in street arrests nearby. Two counties (Del Rio/Val Verde TX, Laredo/Webb TX) read as genuine border-crossing hotspots rather than processing artifacts, since the facility town is itself the crossing point; the rest cannot be told apart from a processing artifact with this data alone.
➲Hover anything — a facility, a route, an airport, a circuit — for its details. Route boxes give the straight-line distance, the time in transit, and an inferred travel mode — which is also how you can tell a real move from an administrative one between buildings on the same site.
ℹThe line at bottom-left reports how many facilities and routes are actually drawn, and how many facilities were dropped for want of coordinates. Read it before drawing conclusions from an empty-looking region.
⟲Reset view returns the layers and the camera to how the map opened.

Moving around the map ×

+ −Scroll or pinch to zoom in for facility-level detail, out for the whole country.
✥Drag to pan across the map.
⇧Shift-drag (or right-click drag) to tilt and rotate into a 3D view -- useful for reading arc height where routes overlap.
⊙Double-click to zoom toward a point.
▲The compass shows which way is north once you've rotated; click it to level out and face north again.
━The scale bar at bottom left gives distance at the current zoom -- worth a glance before judging how far a transfer actually moved someone.
⟲Reset view snaps back to the opening angle, zoom, and layer selection.

Sources ×

Sources. Transfers & facilities: Deportation Data Project, ICE records via FOIA (deportationdata.org). Hold-room coordinates: CovenAnalytica/coldCounter (GitHub), geocoded via the U.S. Census Bureau Geocoder. Federal circuit boundaries: U.S. Census Bureau 2023 cartographic boundary file, circuits per 28 U.S.C. §41. Airports: author's prior flight-mapping work. Flight data: Human Rights First, ICE Flight Monitor (humanrightsfirst.org), joined to this map's airport click panel and flight-route arcs. Hotspot (weekly change): Deportation Data Project apprehension records, county boundaries via the U.S. Census Bureau 2024 cartographic boundary file -- see the "Hotspot" row under "How to read this map" for the method and a real limitation of what this layer can claim. States overlay ×

Two different "state" fields are used throughout this project, never blended: arrests are counted by the state where a person was taken into custody; detainees, detention centers, and time in custody are counted by the state where a detention facility is physically located. A person is very often arrested in one state and detained in another. Full breakdown, including age ranges for both: the "State by state breakdown" page, below.
