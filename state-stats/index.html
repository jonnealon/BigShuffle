State by state breakdown

Arrests, detainees, detention centers, time in custody, and age -- one row per state, compared against each other. Built for a policymaker audience, at the direct suggestion of Becky Wolozin and Melissa Adamson (National Center for Youth Law): a state-level comparison is a real motivator for action in a way a single national number is not.

Two different "state" fields are used here, on purpose, never blended: arrests and their own age figures are counted by the state where a person was arrested; detainees, detention centers, time in custody, and their own age figures are counted by the state where a detention facility is actually located. A person is very often arrested in one state and detained in another -- see "How to read this page" below before comparing the two columns to each other.

How to read this page

Arrest state vs. facility state. "Arrests" (and the arrest-side age range) count where a person was taken into custody. Every other column counts where a detention facility is physically located -- a state can show a large arrest count and a small detainee count (people arrested there are usually transferred elsewhere), or the reverse (a state with major detention infrastructure but comparatively few arrests of its own, like Louisiana).

Median, not average, time in custody. How long a single stint at one facility lasts is heavily skewed by a small number of very long stays -- a plain average would be pulled upward and describe a "typical" stay worse than the median does. Nationally, the median stint is .

Age is approximate. Computed as the event's own year minus birth year -- neither field carries a month or day, so this can be off by up to a year in either direction. Extreme values are shown as the data actually has them, not clipped to a "plausible" range.

"Guantánamo Bay, Cuba" (postal-style code CU in the underlying data) is not a US state -- it is kept as its own row because three real, active detention facilities are located there, with no corresponding arrest-state figures (no one is arrested in Cuba under this framework).
