Circuit-to-circuit flow and outcome

Does a case's outcome track where it was arrested, or where it ends up being decided? For every detained stay in the study window, this compares the federal judicial circuit of arrest against the circuit of the facility where the stay concluded, and checks whether crossing from one to the other predicts removal.

Full method, population, and every caveat: the book's "Circuit-to-circuit flow" chapter section and data/build_circuit_flow_outcomes.py. This page restates the essentials, not a substitute for either. Caveats

  • "Final circuit" is a proxy for the adjudicating circuit, not a record of it. It is the circuit of the last facility in a stay -- the most plausible stand-in for where a video hearing's jurisdiction attaches, but an assumption, not a confirmed fact about any specific case.
  • This does not establish intent. A transfer log cannot distinguish deliberate venue selection from a bed-space or logistics policy that happens to concentrate capacity in the destination circuit's own states -- both predict the identical observed flow.
  • Origin circuit relies on arrest state, not county -- coarser than the county-level arrest geocoding used elsewhere in this project, since DDP does not provide a finer apprehension-circuit field directly.
  • Coverage gap: an arrest in a foreign country, an unmapped U.S. territory (Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands), "Armed Forces -...", or "Other" gets no origin circuit and is excluded from every chart and table here, not guessed.
  • A facility's own circuit-shift rate is reported as a pattern to ask about, not a finding of wrongdoing -- the same framing this project already applies to the ARFF index-mismatch flag and the no-Part-139-certification finding.
